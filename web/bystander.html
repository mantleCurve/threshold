Skip to call 911

Read this first

You will not be arrested for calling 911 to save someone's life.

Your state's Good Samaritan law protects the person who calls and the person who overdosed from drug-possession charges. Calling is the right thing to do and it is the safe thing to do. Stay with them.

Call 911 Tap here — then put it on speaker and keep reading

Say exactly this

"Someone is not breathing. I think it's an overdose. I need an ambulance." Then give the address. Do not hang up.

What to do, in order

  1. Check if they respond

    Shout their name. Rub your knuckles hard on their breastbone. If they don't wake up, keep going.

  2. Give naloxone

    Tilt the head back. Put the nozzle in one nostril until your fingers touch the nose. Press the plunger all the way. That's the whole dose — one nostril, one press.

    If nothing changes in 2–3 minutes, give a second dose in the other nostril. You cannot hurt someone by giving naloxone when they don't need it.

  3. Breathe for them

    Tilt the head back, pinch the nose, and give one breath every five seconds. Follow the circle below.

    5

    Ready

    One breath every five seconds. Watch their chest rise.

  4. Put them on their side

    Once they're breathing on their own, roll them onto their side with the top knee forward and the head tilted back. This stops them choking if they vomit.

  5. Stay until help arrives

    Naloxone wears off before the opioid does. They can stop breathing again. Do not leave, and do not let them sleep it off alone.
