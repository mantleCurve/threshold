1. Describe the changes/updates made in the deployed version

The deployed version turns Threshold into a complete recovery and overdose-prevention platform instead of a single emergency button.

• Added a six-level, always-visible escalation ladder from Baseline to Unresponsive. Safety classification is deterministic and auditable; an AI model never decides the user’s emergency tier.
• Added a focused emergency takeover experience with one-tap 911 access, naloxone instructions, a personalised emergency script, bystander mode that works without an account, and a clear option to rescind a false alarm.
• Added prevention features for ordinary days, including conversational check-ins, craving support, tolerance-loss warnings after detox/hospitalisation/incarceration, refusal and exit scripts, grounding guidance, and a Memory Vault of real caregiver recordings.
• Added complete member and caregiver authentication flows. Registration now requires full name, email address, phone number, and password. Email ownership is verified using a short-lived code delivered through Resend; phone OTP verification is intentionally not required.
• Added consent-based caregiver linking through invite codes. Members control what a caregiver can see below the emergency threshold, can revoke access, and receive clear explanations of every sharing boundary.
• Added real emergency email delivery to verified linked caregivers while keeping sensitive clinical details out of the email.
• Hardened privacy and security with scrypt password hashing, signed secure sessions, server-side caregiver visibility checks, API-key redaction, strict authentication on private actions, bounded inputs, and explicit AI/offline states.
• Improved the public website and account entry points with a clearer homepage, prominent Sign in/Create account/Caregiver registration actions, responsive layouts, accessible focus states, high-contrast emergency controls, and typography aligned with the supplied Threshold design direction.
• Expanded automated coverage across triage, authentication, API routes, storage, privacy isolation, invitations, email verification, caregiver access, generative fallbacks, and voice behavior.

2. Mention the Gen AI services utilized in the submission, and where they were utilized

Google Gemini through OpenRouter

Threshold uses Google Gemini models through the OpenRouter API for language generation only. Gemini is used to:

• generate short, supportive conversational check-in responses;
• prepare a personalised 911 call script from information the member entered in advance;
• create refusal and exit lines in the member’s preferred speaking style;
• phrase tolerance-loss warnings in clear, non-judgmental language;
• produce concise caregiver situation briefs after a deterministic emergency event; and
• select the most contextually relevant real recording from the Memory Vault and explain the selection.

The fast Gemini model is used for interactive or short responses, while the deeper model path is used for prepared emergency scripts and caregiver briefs. Every live generation is identified as live. If OpenRouter is unavailable, the interface explicitly identifies the fallback instead of presenting cached text as a fresh AI response.

ElevenLabs

ElevenLabs Text-to-Speech is used to read guidance, check-in responses, grounding prompts, and emergency instructions aloud. The deployed voice path uses an expressive model for normal speech and a low-latency model for urgent instructions so emergency guidance starts quickly. Stock narration is available by default, and any supporter voice requires separate, recorded supporter consent plus explicit member selection. Real Memory Vault recordings are played as recorded and are never silently converted into synthetic speech.

Resend is also used for transactional email verification and caregiver emergency notifications. It is an email delivery service, not a Gen AI service.

Important safety boundary

Gen AI is deliberately kept outside the safety-critical decision path. The escalation tier, emergency thresholds, caregiver visibility rules, and legal/Good Samaritan information are deterministic or human-reviewed. AI adapts language and presentation; it does not decide whether a person is in medical danger.
