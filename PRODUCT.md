# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Threshold serves people in recovery or at risk of overdose, the caregivers they
explicitly invite, and bystanders responding to a possible overdose. People may
use it while calm, distressed, intoxicated, panicking, half-awake, or unable to
type. The primary task is to understand the current escalation tier and take the
next safe action without unnecessary disclosure.

## Product Purpose

Threshold is a prevention-first recovery and overdose-response product. A
deterministic escalation ladder converts user-controlled settings and observable
signals into an auditable tier. Generative AI may adapt language, but never
decides safety state, legal guidance, or dispatcher-critical facts. Success means
the right person receives a truthful, privacy-scoped intervention quickly, while
the member remains willing to use the product honestly.

## Brand Personality

Calm, candid, protective. Threshold should feel like a prepared person speaking
plainly under pressure: never clinical for its own sake, never cheerful about
danger, and never more confident than the system's evidence.

## Anti-references

- Panic-button interfaces that become useful only after a crisis has started.
- Surveillance products that expose low-tier disclosures to caregivers by
  default.
- Gamified sobriety trackers, streaks, scores, badges, or moralizing language.
- Medical dashboards that require dense reading during an emergency.
- AI interfaces that disguise fallback copy, invent completed actions, or imply
  a human is present.

## Design Principles

1. Truth before reassurance: state exactly what happened, what is planned, and
   what failed.
2. Privacy is enforced before bytes leave the server, never by client-side
   hiding.
3. As danger increases, choices and visual noise decrease.
4. The member owns the ladder below the disclosed emergency threshold.
5. Critical actions work without AI and degrade locally without silence.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG 2.2 AA. All critical guidance must be available visually and
audibly, without relying on color alone. Emergency actions require large touch
targets, visible focus, keyboard and assistive-technology support, reduced-motion
behavior, and local fallbacks for unavailable microphone, speech, location, or
network APIs.
